# Spatial semantics

FEN notation uses ordinary files/ranks only at the state I/O boundary. Internally squares are integer pairs for sensing and structural transformations; learned descriptors use relative displacements and relative edge distances. Piece/position IDs name observations and are never rule constants.

Positive global x is east; positive global y is north. The eight compass directions are ordered independently of board origin. Owner-relative frames preserve lateral x and flip y for Black, making forward positive. Distance is Chebyshev magnitude for geometric descriptors; the signed offset is always retained. A ray is structural alignment along an axis or a diagonal. Its intermediate cells exclude both endpoints and are ordered from source to destination. The adapter records every intermediate occupancy category; it never reduces them to a Boolean `clear` result.

Rook and bishop candidates cover four directions at distances 1–7; queen reuses both families. Knight has eight signed leaps. King has eight unit offsets plus two structural castle proposals. Pawn has forward one/two and forward-diagonal proposals in its own frame, including all four promotion replacements at the destination edge. Enumeration does not inspect blockers, allegiance, king safety or rights. Out-of-board candidates are retained with boundary observations and no structural successor.

The starting-rank feature is a pawn's distance from its owning-side edge. Castling rights and en-passant targets are explicit state observations. Structural castling relocates the piece at the relevant board boundary into the post-move rook cell without asserting that it was a legal rook move. En passant removes the pawn-adjacent square when the input target matches the proposed diagonal. Both operations are intentionally applicable to proposals the oracle would reject. Full king-count and invalid-state correctness is not claimed.

Translations must preserve all relative observations, board boundaries, pawn edge context and history. The transform helper rejects nonempty castling rights or EP targets rather than assuming symmetry. A candidate whose boundary/pawn context changes is excluded from invariance claims. File reflection is tested as an involution and rejects castling rights. Rank reflection plus color/turn flip preserves owner-relative forward and maps EP targets; castling is rejected pending explicit mapping.

`compare_transforms` checks context equality, raw TVs, matched rule IDs, and proof identity modulo renamed state/move IDs. It reports excluded candidates separately and fails on truncation. Tests also rename all piece IDs and inspect pre-hash descriptors for absolute square literals. The controlled transfer fixture moves the entire local configuration one file east; pawn orientation examples separately cover both owners.
